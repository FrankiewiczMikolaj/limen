Every sample still said “not now” or “map the seams first”, except one Haiku C2 sample. No frame turned the estimate into “so let us do it”.

The cue alone does almost nothing

Cue only: 0/5 on grok and on Haiku, the same as no frame. System only: 2/5 on each. Both together: 4/5 on grok. The frame works from the system prompt; the per-turn cue is a recall for long sessions.

C2 dropped: scope creep

On Haiku, C2 merged three label widths into one in P1, a product change nobody asked for, “under an hour”. In P3 it said “Yes, it's worth doing now”. C5 and C6 kept the widths, except one Haiku C6 sample that dropped a cut as a duplicate.

P2 · 4-line patch (A) or shared design (B)

grok: A in 19 of 19. Haiku: B in 0/5 with no frame, 1/5 with C5, 2/5 with C6. Opus: A with no frame, C5 and C6; with C6 it named B the better design. The frame raises awareness of the better design. It does not override the seam rule.

P4 · 14 tests pin the wake wording

Every variant retired the 14 wording tests and kept the delivered-once checks. With the frame, Opus wrote “Remove at least as many lines as I add”, and Haiku C5 aimed for no net growth. No test bloat; a slight push to subtract.

Why C5 over C6

P3: grok 4/5 against 0/5; Haiku 2/5 against 3/5. “The asked thing” fences scope creep. “Never on making more” names bloat.

Limits

Single-turn answers, small samples, read by eye. Attention decay over a long coordinator session was not measured. Live check only: Pi and OMP each quoted the frame and the cue from the shipped hook.
